# Trend Forecast Report: Markets, Macro, Fiscal, Technology and Military Outlook After the 3 November 2026 Midterms (baseline: Trends Journal, 10 Nov 2026)

The Trends Journal's two core premises check out against mainstream reporting: Democrats did win both chambers on 3 November 2026, and the U.S.-Israel war on Iran (begun 28 February 2026) is still unresolved in its ninth month.\[1\] Our base case, though, is an AI-equity bear phase and a stagflation scare that lasts through mid-2027 rather than a full "Dot-Com Bust 2.0". Falling oil prices, a Fed that keeps hiking, and hyperscaler capex that still grows in 2027 (more slowly than consensus) make a 2000-02-scale collapse the less likely path.

## TL;DR

- **Premises confirmed, several numbers corrected.** Democrats hold 54 Senate seats, and AP had them at 254 House seats with 19 races uncalled. The 5 November AI selloff was real: Nasdaq -5.5%, Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) -12.25%, and about $2.3T of Nasdaq-100 market value erased, the index's worst single-day value loss on record (Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg). The Journal's 10 November Nasdaq close is wrong (verified: 25,065.87, not 27,740.80).\[2\] Its Hormuz "-70%" understates the drop in vessel transits but overstates the drop in crude flows.
- **Base case (about 50%), all estimates.** The Fed hikes 25 bp on 9 December to 4.00-4.25% and once more in H1 2027. U.S. CPI peaks near 4% in Q4 2026 and eases to about 2.8% by end-2027 as the oil shock reverses. Brent drifts to about $78 by mid-2027. The S&P 500 bottoms around 6,900 in mid-2027, and the Nasdaq and SOX fall a further 10-20% before stabilising. Hyperscaler capex reaches about $850B in 2027 (consensus: $934.5B) and plateaus in 2028.
- **Main risks.** The bear case (about 25%) is a dot-com-style capex cut combined with a private-credit markdown, taking the Nasdaq toward 14,500 by end-2027. An Iran re-escalation (about 20%) would push Brent above $120. On the fiscal side, the $41.1T debt limit binds in early 2027 and an 11 December funding deadline gives Congress leverage over the war.\[3\] Under divided government, veto overrides are realistic only on bipartisan issues such as data-center ratepayer protection.

### 3d. Energy and Commodities

**Verified.** Brent started 2026 near $60, peaked near $130 in March-April, and settled at **$91.49** on 10 November after touching $89.16, its first sub-$90 print since August (CNBC, Bloomberg).\[4\]\[58\] The drop followed Trump's remarks that more strikes were unnecessary. Saxo's Ole Hansen says crude flows are nearly back to pre-war levels, but LNG and refined products are still tight.\[59\]

**Hormuz is a contested metric.** The Journal's "-70%" sits between two very different measures:
- **Vessel transits:** Iran SITREP (9 November) reports a 7-day average of 5% of pre-war transits and 3% for tankers. IMF PortWatch logged 1 transit on 1 November against a baseline of about 85 a day. UKMTO says traffic is "well below normal but not stalled", and counts are low because many ships switch off transponders.
- **Oil volumes:** preliminary Kpler-based estimates reported around 21 October (WSJ) put flows, including transfers in the Gulf of Oman, at about 14 mb/d, or 82% of pre-war.
- **LNG:** 31 cargoes transited in October, a post-war high (Kpler).\[60\]
- **Security:** the IMO has confirmed 129 incidents and the UN counts 25 seafarers killed. Iran's tanker mine claims for 5-11 November are mostly unconfirmed.\[61\]\[62\]

- **Assessment:** the Journal's forecast 32 is medium plausibility. We put the probability of at least one AI or data-center override by end-2028 at 25%.

## Caveats

- **Single-source baseline.** About a third of the Journal's figures could not be independently checked (Appendix A, final row). We treat them as indicative, and no headline forecast rests on them alone.
- **Contested metrics.** Hormuz "traffic" changes sign depending on whether vessels or barrels are counted. Gaza control figures depend on the definition used. Some exact casualty figures come from partisan outlets reprinting ministry data.
- **Event risk.** The Iran war, the 11 December deadline and the January-February capex guidance can each move most of these forecasts at once. Revisit the register after each.
- **Forecasts are estimates.** All forward values and probabilities are our judgment. The Journal's editorial characterisations are reported as its opinions and restated neutrally where assessed.
